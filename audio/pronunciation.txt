Pronunciation rules (Israeli Hebrew, as taught in kindergarten). Follow them exactly.
Hebrew letter names:
אָלֶף = A-lef, stress on the first syllable.
בֵּית (also written בֶּת) = BET, one syllable, like the English word "bet". No "i" or "y" sound.
גִּימֶל = GI-mel, stress on the first syllable.
דָּלֶת = DA-let, stress on the first syllable.
הֵא = HE, one short syllable.
וָו = VAV.
זַיִן = ZA-yin, stress on the first syllable.
חֵית (also written חֶת) = KHET, one syllable, throaty kh. No "i" or "y" sound.
טֵית (also written טֶת) = TET, one syllable. No "i" or "y" sound.
יוּד = YUD.
כַּף = KAF.
לָמֶד = LA-med, stress on the FIRST syllable (not la-MED).
מֵם = MEM.
נוּן = NUN.
סָמֶךְ = SA-mekh, stress on the first syllable.
עַיִן = A-yin, stress on the first syllable.
פֵּא = PE, with a clear P.
צָדִי = TSA-di, stress on the first syllable.
קוּף = KUF (not "kof").
רֵישׁ (also written רֶשׁ) = RESH, one syllable. No "i" or "y" sound.
שִׁין = SHIN.
תָּו = TAV.
סוֹפִית = so-FIT, stress on the last syllable (as in "מֵם סוֹפִית").
All other words: normal modern Israeli pronunciation and stress, following the niqqud.
